## B01 — BLUF (BrutalistHesitantWriter)

Clawd Closes the Loop is a small platformer where finishing the level means closing an agentic loop: ordered tasks, with bugs, malware and vague instructions in the way. One slice exists and is tested. The loop is a proposal. The pitch is honest about which is which.

## B02 — WHAT EXISTS (GodotDesignBoard)

Slide two is the only screenshot in the deck, because it is the only game. First Steps, in Godot: Clawd at spawn, one step, three spikes, one gap, a finish off to the right. Move, one jump, retry, results. Twenty-five mechanics checks and nine keyboard checks pass. Nobody has played it. Everything after this slide is dashed.

*[On screen: The one real capture with two status cards]*


## B03 — VISION AND PILLARS (GodotDesignBoard)

The vision line: I am the agent, I close the loop. A worker who finishes despite conditions, not a superhero. Four pillars hold it up. Two are already in code: read the landing, earn another try. Two are new: close the loop, and small complete game. When the new ones collide with the old ones, the old ones win. Vague paths hide which way, never where you land. Malware undoes one task, names it, and leaves the station reachable.

*[On screen: Vision line with four pillar cards]*


## B04 — THE LOOP (GodotDesignBoard)

The loop at three scales. Micro is what you already have: read, commit, land or fail with a name. Meso is the pitch: enter, task A, B, C, the gate opens, the flag. Malware sends the last task back to open; a wrong branch loops to the fork. Macro is one session: finish, replay cleaner, stop. Strip the theme and it is still ordered checkpoints plus a patrol. Still a game. That is the honesty test, and it passes on paper only.

*[On screen: Loop diagram with three scale cards]*


## B05 — OBSTACLES (GodotDesignBoard)

Three obstacles, one per column. Bug: the spikes that exist, stationary, cost the attempt. Malware: a patrol that costs the attempt and re-opens your last task. Vague path: a sign that is true but incomplete, and the wrong branch only costs time. Read the bottom rows. What each one may not do is the actual design: no oversized colliders, no undoing two tasks, no hazards on the wrong branch, no hidden landings.

*[On screen: Obstacle taxonomy with three cards]*


## B06 — LEVEL ONE (GodotDesignBoard)

Level one over the geometry that exists. Solid is first steps dot json at two pixels per pixel. Dashed is the proposal: task A on the first step, task B on top of the block, C after the last gap, a patrol between B and C, a fork above the spikes. Every dashed coordinate is a placeholder. The current level is nine hundred sixty wide and has no room for a real fork; the proposal extends past the flag. Nothing here has been simulated.

*[On screen: Level flow over real geometry]*


## B07 — SCOPE (GodotDesignBoard)

The slide with the big number. Fifty-three percent: eight of fifteen features are core, and Zelda's line is forty. She tried twice to get under it and could not without removing the loop. So the deck does what the document does: it hands Bear two options. A reduced MVP with tasks but no gate, no malware, no fork. Or keep the core list and extend a timeline that does not exist yet. Nobody chose. That is correct.

*[On screen: Feature map with the two options]*


## B08 — RISKS AND QUESTIONS (GodotDesignBoard)

Three risks and eight questions, compressed. The risk that can kill it: malware undo reads as punishment. The risk that is already true: zero human playtests. The production risk: fifty-three percent core, no date. The questions that gate the code: does undo feel like a setback, and cut or extend. Zelda's own first fix is on the last slide: answer those two, then greybox the undo and watch one person play.

*[On screen: Risks and questions as four cards]*
